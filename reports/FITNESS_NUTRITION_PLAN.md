## 3. TDEE — How It's Calculated

Working number: **~2,900 cal/day**, from the average of four methods:

| Method | Value |
|---|---|
| Lose It EER (profile) | ~2,915 |
| Mifflin-St Jeor × 1.55 | ~2,860 |
| Katch-McArdle × 1.55 | ~2,890 |
| **Back-calculated from real data** | **~2,870** |

**Two rules:**
1. Use the **×1.55 activity multiplier** ("daily/intense 3–4×/wk") — one notch *below* raw activity level. Higher multipliers over-credit very active people because activity gets compensated.
2. The **back-calculated method is ground truth** (avg intake + weight change × 3500 ÷ days). Short windows (<3 weeks) read high when water is clearing after a blowout — prefer long windows.

**Important caveat:** TDEE is not constant. It swings **~2,450 (low-activity week) to ~3,150 (big hike/ride week)**. Proof: W28 and W29 had nearly identical intake (2,476 vs 2,452), but W29's steps collapsed to 2,606/day and **weight went up 1.2 lb**. Activity isn't a bonus on top of TDEE — it *is* the TDEE.

### Protein — why 180 g
Derived, not arbitrary. Both methods agree at ~30% body fat:
- **Total bodyweight:** 96 kg × 1.6–2.2 g/kg = 154–211 g
- **Lean mass:** ~148 lb LBM (67 kg) × 2.3–3.1 g/kg (Helms, for a deficit) = 154–208 g

At 57, **anabolic resistance** means you need more per meal than a younger lifter: **~40 g × 4 meals** beats the same total in two sittings.

### Fiber — the leading indicator
Fiber is not just a health metric — in your data it **predicts the calorie blowouts**. Low-fiber days line up with high-calorie days (9/8: 11 g fiber / 3,865 cal; 9/13: 7 g fiber / 2,295 cal). When fiber lapses, calories follow within days.

**Sources that work for you:** ground flax in the morning shake, raspberries with Oikos Greek yogurt, vegetables at dinner.
**Avoid:** psyllium/Metamucil powder — it bulks without moving and makes constipation worse for you. Also avoid MiraLAX; combining it with a fast fiber ramp caused the 7/23 diarrhea/dehydration episode. **Ramp fiber slowly** (~5 g every few days).

## 5. The Behavioral Rules (Earned From the Data)

These are the findings that actually moved the needle:

1. **No cheat days.** Every day is measured against 2,000 — including date nights. One 4,500-cal day erases ~2.5 days of deficit.
2. **Alcohol anchors every blowout.** 7/17 (whiskey, 3,903), 8/7 (4,507), 8/15 (piña colada + beer = 898 cal, 5,314 total). Removing the drink removes the day.
3. **Don't crash-restrict afterward.** Eating 520 cal the day after a blowout (8/16) sets up the next binge and costs muscle. Return to 2,000 and move on — the damage is already priced in.
4. **The 9 p.m. kitchen is the real problem**, not the restaurant. The M&Ms + Chipwich (760 cal) did more damage than the dinner.
5. **Bank activity before a known big meal.** A 2.5-hr hike (~1,340 cal) absorbs a 3,300-cal dinner. Ceiling is ~1,500 — you cannot out-hike a 2,500-cal overage.
6. **Judge by the 7-day weight average**, never a single morning. Daily swings ±2–3 lb from sodium, carbs, alcohol.
7. **Exercise funds the TDEE; the fork funds the deficit.** 500 cal takes 5 minutes to eat and 1–2 hours to burn.

## 7. Training Plan

### Structure
- **3–4 strength sessions/week** + near-daily cardio
- **10–20 hard sets per muscle per week**, each muscle **2×/week**
- **RIR 1–3** — *not* failure. Research shows stopping 1–3 reps short produces equal hypertrophy with less fatigue and injury risk.
- **Double progression:** add reps within a range first, then increase load. Do not let Fitbod's weight-first logic drive it.

### Your loading philosophy — validated
Going lighter for more reps is **evidence-based**, not a compromise. Hypertrophy is equal across ~30–85% of 1RM *when sets are taken near failure*. Using Fitbod's suggested reps as a **floor** and pushing to 1–3 RIR is correct execution.

**One addition:** keep some moderately heavy work (5–8 reps) on **safe, stable movements** (leg press, machines, hip thrust). Max strength and bone density respond to load specifically — and both matter at 57.

### Left shoulder (rotator cuff repair)
The shoulder, not the chest, is the limiter on dumbbell pressing — which means those sets never reach chest failure anyway. Fix it with **exercise selection**, not courage:

- **Primary chest driver: Hammer Strength chest/incline press** (fixed path, safe bail, no cuff stabilization demand). Push these to 0–1 RIR.
- **Secondary:** machine fly, cable crossover, push-ups (scapula moves freely).
- **If using dumbbells:** neutral grip (removes external rotation — the provocative component), elbows 30–45°, **stop the descent at torso level**, scapula retracted. A floor press enforces the depth limit mechanically.
- **Keep the prehab.** 169 sets external rotation / 166 internal / 80 face pulls is genuinely diligent — it's what builds the resilience to press confidently.

### Recurring gaps to watch
| Muscle | Issue |
|---|---|
| **Rear delts** | Fixed three times, drifts back to ~2 sets/wk each time. **3–4 sets every upper day.** |
| **Core** | ~2 sets/wk |
| Back | Hovers at 8–9 sets/wk — keep a vertical pull (lat pulldown) in rotation |
| Shoulders | Was over-trained at 17/wk, now balanced at ~9.5. Don't let it creep back. |

### Cardio
Walking (daily), cycling, hiking — this is what funds the ~2,900 TDEE. **Floor: 8,000 steps/day, 3 lifts, and one long session (hike or 12+ mi ride) weekly.**

**Running:** being reintroduced after a 6-month layoff. **3–4×/week max, not daily** — at 211 lb and 57, daily running from a detrained base invites Achilles/calf/plantar injury. Never on leg day. Easy pace (~12–13 min/mi) is fine within 90 min of bed; save intervals for daytime (3–4 hr buffer before sleep).
